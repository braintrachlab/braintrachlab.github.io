name: Andreas Vesalius
born: 1514
year: 1543
life: 1514 – 1564
portrait: pioneers/vesalius.webp
text: In the *Fabrica* (1543) drew the first accurate views of the corpus callosum and of the white matter of the hemispheres, from human dissection.
figure: Horizontal section through the hemispheres, opening the lateral ventricles within the white matter
image: pioneers/fig-vesalius.jpg
source: A. Vesalius, *De humani corporis fabrica libri septem*, Book VII (Basel: Oporinus, 1543)
ref: Catani & Sandrone, *Brain Renaissance: From Vesalius to Modern Neuroscience* (Oxford University Press, 2015)

name: Raymond Vieussens
born: 1641
year: 1684
life: 1641 – 1715
portrait: pioneers/vieussens.webp
text: Named the *centrum ovale* (1684): the white matter as a structure of its own, continuous with the internal capsule.
figure: The fibres of the centrum ovale converging through the internal capsule towards the brainstem
image: pioneers/fig-vieussens.jpg
source: R. Vieussens, *Neurographia universalis*, Tabula XVI (Lyon, 1684); engraving by Beaudeau, Montpellier
ref: *Terminologia Neuroanatomica* (FIPAT 2017), endnote 131

name: Félix Vicq d'Azyr
short: Vicq d'Azyr
born: 1748
year: 1786
life: 1748 – 1794
portrait: pioneers/vicq-dazyr.webp
text: Cut the brain in thin horizontal and coronal slices and printed them in colour plates of new precision; described the mammillothalamic tract that bears his name and the white stripe of the visual cortex.
figure: Horizontal section in colour: grey and white matter distinguished, the ventricles opened
image: pioneers/fig-vicq-dazyr.jpg
source: F. Vicq d'Azyr, *Traité d'anatomie et de physiologie* (Paris: Didot, 1786)
ref: Parent, *Can J Neurol Sci* 2007 — Félix Vicq d'Azyr: anatomy, medicine and revolution

name: Johann Christian Reil
born: 1759
year: 1809
life: 1759 – 1813
portrait: pioneers/reil.webp
text: Hardened brains in alcohol and dissected the fibre bundles: the first descriptions of the arcuate fasciculus, the insula and the corona radiata.
figure: The base of the brain dissected to show its fibre systems
image: pioneers/fig-reil.jpg
source: J. C. Reil, *Archiv für die Physiologie* 11 (1812), Tab. XIII; drawing by Gier, engraving by Schröter
ref: Catani & Mesulam, *Cortex* 2008 — the arcuate fasciculus and the disconnection theme

name: Karl Friedrich Burdach
born: 1776
year: 1822
life: 1776 – 1847
portrait: pioneers/burdach.webp
text: Gave the names still in use: arcuate fasciculus, cingulum, uncinate fasciculus, longitudinal fasciculi.
figure: A hemisphere dissected to expose the radiating fibres of the white matter
image: pioneers/fig-burdach.jpg
source: K. F. Burdach, *Vom Baue und Leben des Gehirns*, Tafel VII (Leipzig, 1819–1826)
ref: Catani & Mesulam, *Cortex* 2008

name: Friedrich Arnold
born: 1803
year: 1838
life: 1803 – 1890
portrait: pioneers/arnold.webp
text: Dissected the fibre systems of the brainstem and hemispheres in fine detail; the frontopontine fibres of the cerebral peduncle are still called Arnold's bundle.
figure: Dissections of the hemisphere in horizontal and medial views, following the course of the fibre systems
image: pioneers/fig-arnold.jpg
source: F. Arnold, *Tabulae anatomicae*, Fasc. I: *Icones cerebri et medullae spinalis*, Tab. X (Zürich, 1838); [UB Heidelberg](https://doi.org/10.11588/diglit.11220#0046)
ref: Catani & Thiebaut de Schotten, *Atlas of Human Brain Connections* (Oxford University Press, 2012)

name: Pierre Louis Gratiolet
born: 1815
year: 1854
life: 1815 – 1865
portrait: pioneers/gratiolet.webp
text: Traced the optic radiations from the thalamus to the occipital lobe, and mapped the folds of the cortex in humans and other primates.
figure: Anatomie comparée du système nerveux (with Leuret, 1839–57): the optic radiations
image:
ref: Catani & Thiebaut de Schotten, *Atlas of Human Brain Connections* (Oxford University Press, 2012)

name: Theodor Meynert
born: 1833
year: 1872
life: 1833 – 1892
portrait: pioneers/meynert.webp
text: Classified the fibres as association, commissural and projection, and described the short U-fibres between neighbouring gyri.
figure: Meynert's diagram of association, commissural and projection systems
image:
ref: *Terminologia Neuroanatomica*, endnote 134

name: Camillo Golgi
born: 1843
year: 1873
life: 1843 – 1926
portrait: pioneers/golgi.webp
text: The 'black reaction' (1873): silver impregnation that shows a whole neuron with its axon, opening the study of the fibres at the cellular level. Nobel Prize with Cajal, 1906.
figure: Golgi's drawing of neurons and their axons stained with the black reaction
image:
ref: 

name: Ludwig Lichtheim
born: 1845
year: 1885
life: 1845 – 1928
portrait: pioneers/lichtheim.webp

figure: Lichtheim's house diagram (1885)
image:
ref: Catani & ffytche, *Brain* 2005

born: 1848
year: 1874
life: 1848 – 1905

figure: Wernicke's 1874 diagram of the sensory and motor speech centres and their connection
image:
ref: Catani & ffytche, *Brain* 2005 — the rises and falls of disconnection syndromes
