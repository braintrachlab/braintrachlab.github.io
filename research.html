Research

Our research

From the detailed anatomy of single pathways and their evolution across primates, to their role in disease and the history of how they were discovered.

01

White matter anatomy

We describe the course and organisation of the white matter tracts of the human brain.

02

Comparative neuroanatomy

By comparing the white matter of the human brain with that of non-human primates, we study how connections have changed during evolution and how these changes relate to human unique abilities such as language.

Prefrontal FAT Precentral FAT

The frontal aslant tract (FAT) in the macaque, chimpanzee and human brain, redrawn as streamlines from Citro et al., Nature Communications (2026).

03

Connections in health and disease

By studying the functions of white matter pathways, we investigate how their damage or alteration contributes to the clinical picture of patients with neurological and psychiatric conditions.

Long segment Anterior segment Posterior segment

Individual atrophy patterns of the arcuate fasciculus in patients with primary progressive aphasia, with their word repetition scores: damage to the temporo-parietal cortex and to the indirect pathway (anterior and posterior segments) is associated with impaired repetition. Redrawn as streamlines from Forkel et al., Neurology (2020).

04

History of neuroscience

The lab explores the history of brain anatomy and neuroscience: the scientists, methods and ideas that, over the centuries, led to our current understanding of the brain.
